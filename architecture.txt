Operational event definitions

- Initial contact: transition from visibly airborne to visibly touching the support surface. Store the last definitely airborne and first definitely contacting frames.
- Lift-off: transition from contact to visible separation, bracketed by the last contacting and first airborne frames.
- Next contact: the next airborne-to-contact transition of the SAME physical paw, following lift-off.
- Complete stride: contact -> lift-off -> next contact, with continuous, sufficiently certain physical identity. Duration comes from source timestamps; uncertain boundaries produce timing bounds.

These are visual contact definitions, not measurements of force or weight bearing.

One chosen method: ground-relative velocity and clearance, with a state machine

Use saved RTMPose coordinates plus the corresponding original video frames:

1. Register visible ground texture between frames using OpenCV, masking the dog and people. This supplies ground motion, including camera movement. Reject unreliable registration or unsupported moving-surface conditions.
2. Measure paw displacement relative to that ground motion and projected clearance from a separately specified ground band. Normalize using the existing body reference.
3. Classify consecutive observations as contact_supported, swing_supported, or unknown. Contact requires near-ground position and low ground-relative motion; lift-off requires departure with supporting motion evidence. Use hysteresis and consecutive supporting observations.
4. Emit CANDIDATE contact/lift-off transitions. Assemble candidate strides only within one identity segment.

Missing paws, unresolved identity, or inadequate ground evidence reset the state to unknown; they cannot support a complete stride. Preserve raw observations unchanged. Body-relative speed alone is insufficient because the body moves during stance.

Use one shared rule configuration, frozen against synthetic cases before scoring animal footage. Ground geometry is input calibration, not dog-specific threshold tuning.

Acceptance and stopping rule

- Synthetic contact/lift-off sequences produce expected transitions; camera transformations preserve event boundaries within one sample.
- Stationary paws, isolated spikes, uncertain identity, and unsupported ground motion cannot establish complete strides.
- Offline replay preserves predictions exactly and never loads models.
- Before a real-footage pass: obtain at least three adjudicated strides across two source videos.
- Proposed pilot gate: at least 80% precision and recall for each event type, with matched boundaries within one native frame of the reviewed bracket. Report each video separately; this is preliminary engineering acceptance.
- Stop after one frozen implementation and evaluation. If annotation or detector gates fail, report the blocker without tuning thresholds against these dogs.
